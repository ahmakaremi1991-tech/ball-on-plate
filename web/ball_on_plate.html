About this simulation

What you are looking at

A plate tilted by two hobby servos must bring a rolling ball to a target, using only a webcam (30 frames per second, 90 ms late). The model includes servo speed limit, deadband and backlash, camera noise and lost frames, and the ball's flight and bounce.

The controllers

  • PID*: classic feedback on the camera position.
  • LQR*: optimal state feedback with a Kalman filter that predicts through the delay.
  • MPC*: the same filter, but it re-plans 1.2 s ahead every frame and knows the tilt and servo limits.

The star means robust-tuned on perturbed rigs. They were tuned for the solid ball starting on the plate and going to a fixed point, so drops, hollow balls and paths are new to them.

Things to try

  • Same start, drop height 50 mm, then 250 mm.
  • Height 150 mm, solid, then hollow: the hollow ball keeps bouncing far longer, and it cannot be steered in the air.
  • Near an edge, throw it outwards: does it stay on the plate?
  • Zig-zag at 100 mm/s, then 250 mm/s, with each controller. At 250 mm/s LQR* and MPC* chase the target so hard that they overshoot off the plate, and the run ends there.
  • Press Draw path, click some points, then Simulate.

How far to trust it

This page is a JavaScript copy of a MATLAB/Simulink model. It was compared with Simulink on 45 reference runs without camera noise: ball position differs by at most 0.4 mm (RMS), and bounce counts and the time the ball is lost are the same. With camera noise, mean settling time over the 8 standard starts differs by at most 1.3 %. The MPC solver matches MATLAB's at 60 test states.

It is a simulation, not a measured rig. The ball's bounciness (0.6 solid, 0.85 hollow) and its sliding friction during a bounce (0.4) are assumed values. A rolling ball never lifts off the plate, and the camera has no parallax for a ball in the air.
